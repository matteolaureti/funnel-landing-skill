# Messaging, story, and evidence

Read when drafting messages or diagnosing why an explanation does not support the proposed action.

Use [clear-communication.md](clear-communication.md) for reader-facing wording, page-wide Identify–Outcome–Objection, meaningful variants, explanatory materials and a worked copy example. This reference defines the message elements and evidence relationships.

## Audience and story

Capture the audience's own description of the task, desired change, previous attempts, doubts, and buying criteria. Prefer actual questions, interviews, support material, or observed behavior. Without these, label the audience model as a hypothesis; avoid demographic stereotypes.

**Hook:** make relevance recognizable in the entry context. A question, demonstration, outcome, or familiar difficulty can work. The following content must support the promise. Avoid curiosity that depends on hiding what the offer is.

**Story:** connect the situation to a credible possibility using the elements the decision needs:

| Element | Question | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | Who is this for, in which situation? | Task, constraints, intended use and relevant exclusions. |
| Outcome | What meaningful change is supported? | Concrete before/after with appropriate limits. |
| Mechanism | How does that change happen? | Workflow, demonstration, behavior, or delivery method. |
| Objection | What still creates doubt or hesitation? | Specific answer, tradeoff, condition, or limitation. |
| Evidence | Why believe the explanation? | Demonstration, specification, relevant case, testimonial, or data. |

Identify–Outcome–Objection plus mechanism and evidence is the interpretation used by this skill. Neither a literal narrative nor a fixed order is required.

**Offer:** define deliverable, audience, included scope, price where applicable, time or data commitment, terms, and fulfillment. Separate the paid offer from the resource, meeting, or trial used to evaluate it.

**CTA:** describe the actual next action. Check label, destination, resulting screen, and completed outcome together. A booking click is not a confirmed slot.

## Features and claims

Connect **feature → benefit → outcome → evidence** where supportable. Example: removable compartments → organize different accessories → keep needed objects together → actual photographs and measurements. This does not establish a productivity improvement.

Features can be important evidence of fit. Explain why they matter without deleting necessary detail. Do not infer guaranteed results from a plausible mechanism.

## Match evidence to objections

| Doubt | Relevant response or evidence |
|---|---|
| Physical fit | Dimensions, scale images, capacity and limits. |
| Resource usefulness | Readable preview and completed example. |
| Workflow compatibility | Relevant demonstration and confirmed capabilities. |
| Setup effort | Actual tasks, responsibilities and support. |
| Outcome credibility | Documented observations, context, timeframe and limits. |
| Commitment | Clear cost, included scope and applicable terms. |

For consequential doubts, record response, available evidence, and gaps. A famous logo does not establish results; a support testimonial does not prove performance. If evidence is missing, revise the claim, demonstrate what is observable, or identify the material needed.

## Persuasion and attribution

Cialdini's listed principles are reciprocity, scarcity, authority, commitment/consistency, liking, social proof, and unity. Consistency concerns alignment with prior commitments, not just repeated brand messaging. Empathy can improve relevance but is not an additional principle in this list. [Official overview](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/).

Use an element when it helps evaluation: relevant experience, genuine credentials, actual availability, or useful value. Do not impose a quota. Free value does not entitle a business to a purchase, and free shipping does not prove reciprocity. Avoid fabricated scarcity, guilt, unsupported authority, and unverified endorsements.

[Hook–Story–Offer is described by ClickFunnels](https://www.clickfunnels.com/blog/hook-story-offer/). This skill's decision rules and evidence checks are an operational synthesis; these sources do not guarantee outcomes. Inspirational examples and numerical claims are not verified project facts.
