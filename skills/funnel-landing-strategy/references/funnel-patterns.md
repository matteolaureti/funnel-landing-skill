# Selecting the funnel path

Read when planning or materially revising the architecture. Compare options instead of assigning a standard funnel to every business.

## Decide what must be resolved

Consider knowledge on entry, motivation, existing trust, offer complexity, ability to assess fit, commitment, stakeholders, fulfillment, and the business's capacity. Each additional page, resource, email, or meeting needs a role. A shorter path is not necessarily better if it hides necessary information. Price alone does not determine complexity.

| Pattern | Suitable condition | Illustrative path | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct sale | Fit and terms can be assessed autonomously. | Entry → sales page → checkout → confirmation. | The page must supply practical detail and trust. |
| Resource to paid offer | Immediate task is meaningfully related to the offer. | Entry → resource page → delivery → relevant follow-up → paid offer. | Resource interest may not become purchase intent. |
| Demo | Product evaluation requires contextual explanation. | Entry → demo page → booking → confirmation → demonstration → proposal. | Calendar friction, qualification, and commercial capacity. |
| Consultation | Actual assessment or advice is offered. | Entry → consultation page → relevant qualification → booking → advice → next step. | The meeting must provide the service promised. |
| Autonomous trial | People can verify value through product use. | Entry → trial → activation → meaningful use → paid choice. | Signup does not demonstrate successful evaluation. |

These options can be combined. A trial can provide optional demo help; a direct sale can offer useful guidance without forcing email capture. Do not add a branch just because a tool supports it.

## Evaluate an intermediate offer

Specify immediate value, relevance to the final audience, requested commitment, the additional need the paid offer addresses, and delivery. A resource must be useful on its own. Do not hide the promised resource behind an unexpected purchase or meeting.

A demo shows and evaluates a product; it is not automatically independent advice. A consultation needs an actual advisory scope. A trial must enable meaningful evaluation, with limits and terms clear.

## Model connected steps

Use: **incoming context → unresolved decision → message and evidence → offer and action → resulting state and next step**.

Name the completed result: order, resource delivered, slot confirmed, account activated, or value experienced. Define relevant branches and operational dependencies. Multiple pages are justified by different audiences, offers, or decision roles rather than one page per C. A specific campaign should not send people to a destination where they must reconstruct the offer.

## Check sensitivity to context

For uncertain choices, record a condition that would change the recommendation:

- Customization may require a quotation instead of immediate purchase.
- Someone already seeking the paid offer may skip the resource.
- Meaningful autonomous evaluation may remove the need for a required meeting.
- A generic resource may attract people outside the paid offer's audience.
- Capacity constraints may make the proposed delivery or booking path unworkable.

This keeps a useful default from becoming a universal rule.
