# Clear communication for funnel pages

Read when writing or revising reader-facing copy, developing message variants, or choosing material that makes a promise understandable. Use the agreed strategy and project facts; this reference does not require a new discovery exercise for every edit.

Contents: message foundation; Identify–Outcome–Objection across the page; wording and tone; meaningful variants; worked copy; demonstrations and numbers; review; source boundaries.

## Start with a message the reader can explain

Make the offer's relevance understandable before asking the reader to investigate it. Title and supporting copy together should establish what is offered, who can use it, and the useful change it supports. They need not contain the entire pitch. Let curiosity concern the mechanism, application or evidence of a clear possibility.

Whop recommends a specific buyer benefit in offer headlines and a simple pitch that makes the offer and audience immediately apparent. Adapt those principles to the entry context; do not impose its headline lengths or import its example promises. [Whop: offer headlines](https://whop.com/blog/how-to-write-offer-headlines/), [Whop: simple pitch](https://whop.com/blog/start-a-coaching-business/).

Reuse a compact message foundation from the brief:

| Element | Capture | Illustrative freelance SaaS |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | Person, task, situation and relevant constraint. | A freelancer reviewing client comments on successive proposals. |
| Outcome | A specific change the offer actually supports. | Find the comments and the version the client approved. |
| Objection | A consequential doubt about suitability or commitment. | How are comments connected to a version? What happens during approval? |
| Mechanism | Actual behavior or service delivery. | Proposals, comments and approvals in one workspace. |
| Evidence | What can be inspected or documented. | A working example of one proposal through review and approval. |
| Offer and action | Exchange, terms and completed next step. | A monthly subscription; checkout followed by account access. |

This is an internal writing aid, not customer-facing terminology. Keep material assumptions and gaps visible to the author. Do not turn an unconfirmed capability into a fact by repeating it throughout the page.

For real projects, use customer questions, interviews, support conversations and suitable reviews to learn which situations and terms recur. Copyhackers describes review mining as a way to discover audience motivations, doubts and language rather than inventing the message. [Copyhackers: review mining](https://copyhackers.com/2014/10/amazon-review-mining/).

Whop likewise recommends learning directly from existing customers through questions and feedback, then aligning the message with their needs. Use its audience-research principle without imposing demographic fields that do not explain the decision. [Whop: understand your customer](https://whop.com/blog/market-your-whop/).

For useful excerpts, retain source and context, then note the task, desired change and doubt they reveal. Distinguish a verbatim excerpt from your interpretation. Questions about competitors suggest things to investigate; they do not establish your product's capabilities or your customers' results. Without audience evidence, write with an explicit audience hypothesis rather than fabricated quotes. Research is not permission to contact people or publish their words.

## Use Identify–Outcome–Objection throughout the decision

Treat these three elements as recurring questions, not three mandatory sections or words to repeat. An individual block can answer one question especially well. Across the page, each return should add information, a demonstration or a condition. Offer, price and action remain necessary even when they do not fit the three labels.

| Function | Reader's decision | Possible contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Captivate | Is this relevant to what I need? | Name a recognizable task or supported result; identify the offer in the opening. |
| Curiosity | What would this let me do, and how? | Show one use case, an output preview or a concrete change. |
| Convince | Will it work for my situation? | Explain the mechanism; answer a material objection with relevant evidence or a clear limit. |
| Convert | What exactly am I choosing? | State deliverable, commitment and next step; resolve remaining practical doubts. |

For the freelance SaaS, the opening identifies proposal review. A workflow block shows how comments relate to a proposal. A demonstration shows an approved version. Commercial details explain access and cost. Each develops the same message instead of restating “everything in one place.”

Put answers where the reader needs them. If client participation is central to evaluation, explain it alongside the workflow, after verifying the actual behavior; a distant FAQ is not a substitute. If price is the immediate buying question, make it easy to find. Use the incoming message and audience knowledge to choose the order.

This page-wide application is this skill's operational synthesis of the supplied video and project experience, not a validated universal formula. [Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ab6H-9fxlPI).

## Write plainly without flattening the voice

Use the audience's language. Prefer concrete actions and familiar words; explain necessary unfamiliar terms. Clear language also matters for specialists. GOV.UK's guidance supports familiar vocabulary and active constructions, while allowing useful specialist terminology and exceptions. Its government-specific rules are not requirements for a commercial page. [GOV.UK: clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/).

- Name the object and action: “collect comments on the proposal” communicates more than “simplify your flow.”
- Make the actor apparent when it matters: distinguish what the customer does, what the software does and what the service provider delivers.
- Replace abstract claims with the supported operation. “Optimize collaboration” might become “collect client comments on the same proposal,” if that is what the product does.
- Keep conditions close to the promise. First proposal timing and final delivery timing are different; forecasted cash and actual available cash are different.
- Divide overloaded sentences when this clarifies the decision. Do not enforce a universal word count or remove useful terms to obtain a readability score.
- Preserve necessary limits. Prefer a positive, supported statement to a sweeping promise such as “never have problems again,” but use direct exclusions when they establish fit.
- Keep the same name for the same object. Avoid cycling through “proposal,” “project” and “document” for stylistic variety if they mean different things in the product.

Write as a knowledgeable person explaining the offer to its intended reader. Directness can be warm, confident or restrained. Choose tone from brand and context; a pricing explanation may need more precision than an introductory sentence. Mailchimp distinguishes a relatively stable voice from a tone that adapts to the reader's situation, and prioritizes clarity over entertainment. Borrow that distinction, not Mailchimp's particular humor or personality. [Mailchimp: voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/).

Use metaphors, wit or an evocative brand line when the reader can still understand the offer immediately. Do not make decoding the slogan the price of entry. Avoid invented feelings, blame, theatrical pain amplification and questions whose only purpose is to make the reader agree.

## Create variants by changing the angle

Keep the audience, product behavior, claim strength and commercial terms constant. Change the route into the message. Use whichever angles the task needs; do not generate every type automatically.

These English examples assume the same hypothetical SaaS: proposals, client comments and approvals in a workspace for freelancers. None implies measured time savings or increased sales.

| Angle | Example opening | Useful when |
|---|---|---|
| Task | “Collect client comments on your proposals.” | Visitors already recognize the task. |
| Result | “Find the approved proposal and your client's comments.” | The end state matters most. |
| Situation | “Comments in chat, proposals in email? Bring them into one workspace.” | Scattered information is a supported audience concern. |
| Audience and offer | “A workspace for freelancers: proposals, client comments and approvals.” | Visitors need to identify the product category and its audience. |
| Demonstration | “See how a proposal moves from comments to approval.” | A relevant demonstration is available. |
| Objection | “Which version did your client approve?” | The next content supplies the specific answer; supporting copy identifies the software. |

An evidence-led opening is another option when relevant documented evidence exists. Name the observed result and context; do not insert a percentage to make a weak premise look specific. Objection-led wording is useful only when the concern matters to this audience and the offer can answer it.

Evaluate the whole opening, including its supporting line and visual. A question or demonstration title may need an explicit category in the subheading. Do not force every headline to carry audience, result, mechanism, objection and price at once.

Prefer variants that explore different relevant motives or ways of understanding the offer. Cosmetic synonyms do little to test a message. State what changes and why each candidate may suit the entry context; do not label one “highest converting” without observations.

## Worked copy: one message, several contributions

All product behavior, price and examples below are hypothetical, consistent with the SaaS walkthrough in [worked-examples.md](worked-examples.md). They show editorial application, not actual customer research or conversion performance.

**Opening**

> Collect client comments and find the approved proposal.
>
> FLUSSO is a workspace for freelancers that keeps proposals, client comments and approvals together.

The title gives a task and useful result; the supporting line identifies offer and audience.

**Situation and mechanism**

> Your client comments in chat, you update the proposal, and the new version ends up in email. When you return to the project, you have to piece together what happened.
>
> With FLUSSO, keep the proposal, comments and approval in the same workspace.

The situation is a planning hypothesis to check against the intended audience. The mechanism names only the assumed behavior; it does not promise automatic import from chat or email.

**Demonstration and objection**

> Which proposal did your client approve?
>
> See an example: the initial proposal, the client's comments and the approved version.

Pair with the relevant demonstration. If only an illustrative mockup exists, label it as such; if version association is not implemented, mark that gap in the draft rather than presenting it as a confirmed function.

**Offer and action**

> The monthly plan costs €29.
>
> Buy the monthly plan.

Before publication, supply the verified included scope, billing conditions, cancellation terms and access process. The price is hypothetical; a button label is appropriate only if its destination actually begins that purchase. Do not invent a trial or a cancellation promise to make the action more attractive.

Other concise rewrites from hypothetical drafts:

| Interpretive line | Concrete alternative |
|---|---|
| “Make room for design. Keep your team moving.” | “A designer for your team's social graphics and presentations.” Add the monthly service and capacity in supporting copy. |
| “Set a course for next month.” | “See your expected income and expenses for next month.” Explain the forecast's inputs and limits. |
| “Creativity without limits.” | “Social graphics and presentations, with one active request at a time.” Use actual included work and capacity. |

## Communicate through demonstrations and outputs

Choose material for the question it answers. A relevant image or diagram can explain a relationship that would be cumbersome in prose; it still needs understandable context. NN/G distinguishes readable wording from actual comprehension, and recommends audience-familiar terminology, explanatory visuals and observation of realistic tasks. [NN/G: readability and comprehension](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/).

| Need | Useful material | Keep clear |
|---|---|---|
| Identify the situation | A relevant task example or recognizable input. | Whose task it is; whether the example is hypothetical. |
| Understand the outcome | A sample deliverable or the resulting product state. | What the person receives and what the sample establishes. |
| Understand the mechanism | A short working demonstration or annotated sequence. | Actual behavior, prerequisites and unsupported steps. |
| Assess suitability | Included/excluded work, an actual workflow or a relevant specification. | Conditions and tradeoffs. |
| Address result credibility | A documented case, customer statement or measured observation. | Scope, context and what is not established. |
| Understand commitment | Plan contents, timeline and post-action instructions. | Total relevant cost, responsibilities and fulfillment. |

In the design service, a brief beside the corresponding presentation explains what is delivered. In the cash-planning software, a labeled forecast can explain the calculation; it does not prove improved finances. In the proposal software, a real approval flow can demonstrate behavior; it does not prove increased proposal acceptance.

Place explanatory material near the claim or doubt it supports. Use captions to distinguish actual screens, illustrative mockups, client work and invented samples. Preserve that distinction in the visible artifact, not only in internal notes. Keep essential terms and conclusions available as text rather than only inside a screenshot, animation or video.

Give headings enough meaning to support scanning. NN/G's web-reading research supports informative headings, concise passages and factual wording rather than exaggerated promotion. The study measures usability in its setting, not the conversion effect of this resource. [NN/G: web reading](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/).

## Use numbers for information and evidence

Different numbers do different work:

| Number type | Editorial use |
|---|---|
| Offer specification | State a verified price, deliverable count or meeting duration with its conditions. |
| Observed result | Give the metric, population or case, timeframe, source and relevant baseline. An observation is not automatically a causal effect or typical result. |
| Projection or calculator | Label the estimate and inputs. Keep assumed returns separate from observed outcomes. |
| Illustrative sample | Label fictional data visibly. Do not present it as a testimonial, customer statistic or achieved result. |

For percentages, clarify what changed, compared with what, and over which period. For rate changes, distinguish percentage points from relative change. Explain material qualifications alongside the number. Use [review-criteria.md](review-criteria.md) for counting and commercial measurement.

Without result evidence, communicate a concrete output or demonstrable capability instead. Replace “cut processing time by 40%” with a truthful explanation of the operation if the reduction has not been measured. Evidence specificity can also come from a readable sample, dimensions or an actual process; numerical decoration is not necessary.

## Review understanding and progression

For an editorial pass, inspect the actual copy and materials:

- Can the opening be paraphrased as a concrete offer, audience and utility?
- Does each substantive block add a decision-relevant answer, example, condition or evidence? Repeated action access can have a useful navigational purpose.
- Are subjects, objects and product terms clear? Do headings still communicate when scanned on their own?
- Does each important outcome have a credible mechanism and appropriate support? Are practical objections answered near the relevant claim?
- Did a rewrite introduce stronger certainty, broader scope, a new feature or a numerical promise?
- Do examples and media visibly preserve the distinction between illustration and evidence?
- Does the CTA match the action, commitment and resulting step?

For a comprehension check with intended users, ask neutral questions about the offer, suitability, expected result and next action. Where useful, ask them to find an answer or complete a realistic task, and observe their interpretation. “Is this clear?” or a readability score alone cannot establish comprehension. This is an application of the NN/G comprehension guidance cited above.

An author or model review can catch ambiguity; it is not independent user evidence. Record misunderstandings and revise the affected content. If conversion effectiveness is the question, evaluate the relevant downstream outcome under appropriate conditions; do not turn clear wording into a guaranteed sales result.

## Source boundaries

The linked Whop and Copyhackers material supplies practitioner guidance; Mailchimp and GOV.UK supply organizational writing guidance; NN/G reports usability research and comprehension methods. They support particular principles, not a single jointly validated framework. The recurring Identify–Outcome–Objection model, variant selection and funnel examples are this skill's synthesis. English-language style guidance is adapted to the user's language; do not mechanically import English word limits or a company's tone.

Use this resource directly for writing. Read external sources again only when the task needs further research, a source-specific claim or an update. Do not require browsing for every headline.
