# Use the 4 Cs to build the funnel

The 4 Cs describe what you want to help the reader do: notice the offer, become interested, trust it and take action. The supplied funnel diagram calls these **Get attention**, **Spark interest**, **Build trust** and **Take action**.

## Use the references together

- [Funnel context](funnel-context.md) records the customer, their goals, the problems they want to avoid and the offer.
- [Messaging and Story](messaging-and-story.md) helps you choose what to talk about. Identify keeps the message relevant to that person. An Outcome or Objection gives them a reason to care.
- [Direct communication](direct-communication.md) helps you say that point in words they understand straight away.
- This guide explains what each C should accomplish and shows ways to do it with copy, images, demonstrations and actions.

For each piece of content, decide what the reader needs next. Choose a subject from the Story that serves that purpose, then express it clearly. For example, a result they want can attract their attention; showing how they can reach it can spark their interest and build trust.

## Captivate: get attention

Give the right person a reason to stop and look. Talk about something they want or something they already deal with. A clear result, a familiar problem, a question or an interesting demonstration can do this.

For tutors who teach online, an Outcome could be:

> Earn €300 more a week by teaching 5 extra students.

An Objection could be:

> Stop spending your evenings arranging lesson times by text.

These are different openings for the same audience. Each makes one point. Choose the one that fits the context and the reader's reason for looking.

## Curiosity: spark interest

Give the person a reason to learn more. Show something they would like to achieve, offer a useful preview or begin answering a question the opening raises.

After an opening about evenings spent arranging lessons, a tutor might want to know how students could book without that exchange of messages. You could invite them to try it:

> Try booking a lesson as a student.

An interactive calendar can let them choose a free time and see the booking confirmation. They learn something about the offer by using it.

You could also introduce a short demonstration:

> See how students book a lesson while you're teaching.

Use appealing offers and free resources to encourage people to find out more. A free resource, preview, trial or consultation can serve that purpose. Choose something that helps the person explore the result or solution they care about.

Curiosity should come from wanting to know how it works or what is possible. Keep the meaning of the words clear.

## Convince: build trust

Help the person see why the offer can help them. Show how it works and what people achieve with it. Demonstrations, experience, expertise, reviews and customer results can contribute.

For the tutor, show the process after a student books: the lesson appears in the calendar and the student receives the meeting link. A short explanation could say:

> Students choose from the times you're free. Each booking adds the lesson to your calendar and sends the student the link.

This explains how the product removes the need to agree lesson times by text. The feature has a clear job in the Story.

A customer example can go further. Show how many lessons a tutor taught before and after using the product, or how much time they spent arranging them. Explain the change they cared about: more income, more teaching time or an evening back.

Build trust through expertise, testimonials, results, webinars, sales videos and reviews. Within a landing page, a product demonstration or customer story can do that work. A demonstration may also spark interest, so it can serve Curiosity and Convince together.

## Convert: encourage action

Help the person take the next step. Use a clear action label and explain what they will get, what it costs and what taking the step involves.

For booking software with a €19 monthly subscription, the action could be:

> Subscribe for €19/month

Nearby text can explain what happens next:

> Create your account and add the times you're free. Then share your booking page with your students.

If the offer starts with a free trial, the action could instead be:

> Start your 7-day trial

State the next paid step nearby:

> €19/month after the trial.

The diagram mentions calls to action, incentives and closing an offer. A discount, bonus or trial can encourage action when it fits the offer. Use the action that serves the project: purchase, sign-up, resource download or a booked call.

## Let the purposes overlap

These examples illustrate choices, rather than a four-section page. A landing page can develop attention across its headline and opening explanation. An interactive preview can create interest and show why the product works. A price and action button can be available while the reader explores those details.

The same purposes can also continue across an ad, a landing page, a demo and an email. Decide what the reader needs at each point. Choose the number of sections, amount of detail and combination of media that help them move forward.

Keep talking to the same customer. Let different passages explain different outcomes, problems, product details and reasons to act. The funnel develops the Story as the reader learns more.
