# Project brief and page handoff

Read when collecting context or defining connected pages. Adapt fields to the task; avoid an exhaustive mandatory questionnaire. Keep project facts outside the skill. Reuse the existing brief, update changed decisions, and save only when useful within the authorized project.

## Project context

| Field | Capture |
|---|---|
| Offer | Deliverable, scope, actual price and terms when known. |
| Audience | Situation, task, need, constraints, buying criteria and stakeholders. |
| Desired change | What people want and what the offer can support. |
| Alternatives | Current approach, previous attempts and competing choices. |
| Entry | Source, intent, message seen and prior knowledge or trust. |
| Evidence | Available demonstrations, specifications, cases, testimonials or data. |
| Doubts | Known objections and hypotheses. |
| Business outcome | Purchase, paid activation or another explicitly requested result. |
| Operations | Delivery, capacity, booking, onboarding and relevant dependencies. |
| Existing decisions | Strategy, brand voice, pages and implementation constraints. |

Separate **confirmed facts** with sources or owners, **assumptions** with affected decisions, and **missing information** with consequences and ways to resolve it. Dates matter where facts may have changed.

## Architecture and message

Record path and rationale, local conversions and final outcome, relevant branches, and conditions that would change the recommendation. Capture hook, audience recognition, supported outcome, mechanism, objections, evidence, entry offer if applicable, paid offer and action.

## Page card

| Field | Specify |
|---|---|
| Role | Why the page exists in the journey. |
| Incoming context | Message already seen, source, intention and knowledge. |
| Local conversion | Completed action and relationship to the final outcome. |
| Transition | What remains to understand, believe or verify. |
| Message | Relevant Hook–Story–Offer elements. |
| Evidence | Material answering important doubts; availability and gaps. |
| Structure | Content hierarchy, necessary sections and evidence placement. |
| Action | CTA behavior, fields or selections and requested commitment. |
| Destination | Resulting page or activity, delivery and expectations. |
| Verification | Hypotheses, observations or metrics and interpretation limits. |

The card supports a coherent page; its internal fields and framework names do not belong in product copy unless they help a visitor decide.

## Handoff

Provide the requested copy with editorial notes for missing facts. Separate usable content from content requiring verification. A marked claim gap is not an invented testimonial.

For implementation, include CTA destinations, form outcomes, relevant error and recovery behavior, and continuity on smaller screens. Do not prescribe a technology or layout because it appeared in an example.

For measurement, define observations, counting unit, segment and window as relevant; connect intermediate conversions to downstream outcomes. Keep proposed hypotheses separate from observed results.
