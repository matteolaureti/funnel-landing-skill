# Funnel Landing Strategy

A funnel helps someone go from noticing an offer to taking action. To build it, you need to understand the customer, decide what to say and help them move towards that action.

- **Project context:** Record who the customer is, what they want, what they want to avoid and what the offer does.
- **Story:** Use that information to write about what matters to the customer.
- **4 Cs:** Get their attention, spark their interest, build their trust and encourage them to act.

Use this method for a landing page, social post, ad, video, email or connected campaign.

## The funnel: the 4 Cs

| Part | Purpose | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Captivate** | Get attention. | Give the right person a reason to stop and look: a situation they know, a result they want or a problem they want to avoid. |
| **Curiosity** | Spark interest. | Make them want to know more. Show what they could achieve, explain something useful or give them a preview of the offer. |
| **Convince** | Build trust. | Help them believe the offer can work for them. Show it in use, explain how it works or share customer experiences and results. |
| **Convert** | Encourage action. | Tell them what to do next, what they will get and what it costs or involves. An incentive can help them take that step. |

The 4 Cs guide the reader's progress. They do not set the number of sections. Attention can carry across several sections. A demonstration can spark interest and build trust at the same time. The reader can have a way to act before reaching the end. Read [four-cs.md](references/four-cs.md) for explanations and examples of each purpose, and how to use the Story and direct communication within the funnel.

## The Story: what to say

Build the message around three questions:

- **Identify:** Who are you talking to? What are they trying to do, and how are they doing it today? Describe a situation they can recognize.
- **Outcome:** What do they want to achieve or experience? Say what would change for them and why they would care.
- **Objection:** What do they want to avoid while trying to achieve that result today? Look at the problems, effort or unpleasant experiences they already face, before introducing the product.

For example, track athletes want to run faster and win more races. They also want to avoid blisters from running. The shoes enter the message as a way to help them do that.

Identify tells you who you are talking to. In each passage, choose an Outcome or Objection that matters to that person. A message can focus on a result; another can focus on what they want to avoid. Develop those points throughout the funnel. Features explain how the product helps. Combine points where useful, rather than making every message include all three elements.

Read [messaging-and-story.md](references/messaging-and-story.md) when deciding what to say and how to develop it across the content.

## Record the project context

For a new project, create a Markdown context document using [funnel-context.md](references/funnel-context.md). Keep it in the project and reuse it for later content. Update it when you learn something new about the audience or offer.

## Write and build the content

Read [direct-communication.md](references/direct-communication.md) when writing or rewriting copy. It explains how to express the Story with simple words, a natural voice and details the reader can understand straight away.

Choose the sections, words, visuals and interactions that help the reader move through the 4 Cs. Let the project determine the format and length.

Follow the user's requested language and deliverable.
