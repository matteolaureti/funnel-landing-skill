# Funnel Landing Strategy

A funnel moves a potential customer from paying attention to taking action. Use the **4 Cs** to guide that progression, and the **Story** to decide what to say along the way. Ground both in the project's context.

This applies to a landing page, social post, ad, video, email or connected campaign. Choose the format and structure that fit the assignment.

## The funnel: four purposes

| Part | Purpose | What the content should do |
|---|---|---|
| **Captivate** | Get attention. | Make the right person notice something relevant to their situation, a result they want or an experience they want to avoid. |
| **Curiosity** | Spark interest. | Show a desirable possibility and give them a reason to explore it further: an explanation, example, demonstration or appealing offer. |
| **Convince** | Build trust. | Show why the offer can deliver that possibility for them. Use relevant demonstrations, expertise, customer experiences or results. |
| **Convert** | Encourage action. | Make the next action, what they receive and the commitment clear. An incentive can encourage the next step. |

These describe the general progression, not a layout. One purpose can continue across several sections; one section can serve several purposes. Attention can develop into interest, and interest into confidence, within the same passage. An action can remain accessible throughout.

## The Story: what to talk about

Communicate through three connected questions:

- **Identify:** Who is this for? Describe the person, what they are trying to do, their problems and their current approach so they can recognize themselves.
- **Outcome:** What do they want to achieve or experience? Make the desired change concrete.
- **Objection:** What do they want to avoid in their current way of pursuing that outcome? Start with the unwanted experiences, consequences, costs or effort they encounter before introducing the product.

In a shoe shop example, these are track athletes, faster running, and avoiding blisters. These motivations already exist before choosing the shoes. Introduce the offer as a response to the result they want and the downside they want to avoid.

Use these throughout the funnel, wherever they make the message relevant. Features explain how the desired result or avoided downside becomes possible; they should support the story rather than become the whole pitch. Read [messaging-and-story.md](references/messaging-and-story.md) when developing that connection.

## Ground the content in context

For a new funnel project, create a Markdown context document using [funnel-context.md](references/funnel-context.md). Record the audience, desired outcomes, experiences to avoid, offer, capabilities and supporting material. Reuse and update that document for later content.

## Create the content

Choose sections, wording, visuals and interactions that develop attention, interest, trust and action. There is no required section count or catalogue of funnel patterns.

Follow the requested language and deliverable.
